# Morocco drought comparison

Two original, unchanged NASA Earth Observatory JPEGs show the same area around
Casablanca on **12 February 2023** (Aqua MODIS) and **19 February 2024** (Terra
MODIS), 372 days apart during the winter rainy season. Each is 2045 × 1363 pixels.
`case.json` records their direct download URLs, acquisition dates, dimensions,
SHA-256 hashes, a suggested question, and one dated contextual reference.

Source: NASA Earth Observatory, [Drought Parches Morocco](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/drought-parches-morocco-152467/),
published 22 February 2024. The reference text is a 118-word paraphrase of the
article, not a direct quotation. The JPEGs were copied byte for byte from the
downloaded originals; they were not cropped, recolored, resized, or generated.

## Comparison limits

The matching coastline and landscape support a regional visual comparison.
Scientific pixel-level co-registration and identical viewing/illumination
conditions have not been established. The platforms and acquisition dates differ;
the earlier scene contains clouds in part of the southwest. Drought was already
present in 2023, so the earlier image is not a healthy baseline.

The JPEGs are regional visualizations, not calibrated reflectance, parcel crop
measurements, or soil tests. MODIS native resolution varies by band: 250 m for
bands 1–2 and 500 m for bands 3–7 ([NASA specifications](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/about/specifications.php)).
The JPEG pixel dimensions do not establish independent ground detail or a
verified meters-per-pixel scale. Greenness changes alone cannot establish crop
identity, yield loss, reservoir volume, soil nutrient deficiencies, or fertilizer
rates. The article's regional drought explanation is contextual evidence rather
than a diagnosis of any particular field.
